Our core research · the working map

Seven doors, and one trapdoor.

Seven domains where creative work gets stuck—plus one important look-alike that is not a block at all. These are the visible symptoms; we work on what drives them. This is our own synthesis of the maps we found across creative-block research, psychology and practice: not a single published taxonomy, but the working model we built from them.

01 · Execution

Starting & Doing

Intention that will not become action.

The gap between deciding and doing—starting, keeping momentum, and finishing. The work is clear enough; it just will not leave your head and reach the page.

Does this sound like you?

I keep preparing instead of startingProcrastination I can't explainA drawer of unfinished projectsI never quite finish

What is often underneath

Avoidance doing a protective job, or perfectionism raising the cost of a first move until starting feels unsafe.

02 · Identity

Confidence & Exposure

When the work feels like a verdict on you.

The work feels unsafe to show, or like a referendum on your worth rather than a draft. Finishing can feel more dangerous than starting, because finishing means being seen.

Does this sound like you?

Impostor feelings that won't quietFinishing feels dangerousFeedback knocked my confidenceIt must be perfect before anyone sees it

What is often underneath

Identity fusion—self-worth tied directly to output—and a fear of rejection that makes every decision feel like a test of your value.

03 · Direction

Direction & Meaning

Too many directions, or none that pull.

Either too many possible paths and no way to commit, or no reason that still genuinely matters. The engine runs; there is nowhere it wants to go.

Does this sound like you?

Too many ideas, no priorityI've lost why I'm doing thisBigger questions about vocationThe work no longer feels like mine

What is often underneath

Drained motivation, or a values and identity question the work alone cannot answer.

04 · Depletion

Energy & Recovery

The tank is empty.

Capacity, not willpower, is the real problem. The ideas may be there, but there is nothing left to bring them out with.

Does this sound like you?

Nothing flows however hard I pushI'm running on emptyExhausted before I beginI've stopped caring, and that scares me

What is often underneath

Burnout—system-wide fatigue where energy, motivation and emotional capacity collapse together. The block is a late-stage symptom, not the cause.

05 · Process

The Work Itself

A real problem inside the work.

Not fear, not fatigue—a genuine craft or process problem, or a true dead end that needs solving on its own terms.

Does this sound like you?

The idea isn't workingI'm stuck on one approachI can't see another way through

What is often underneath

Fixation on an early idea that crowds out alternatives—or a genuine craft gap worth naming honestly.

06 · Systems

Structure & Workflow

The system trips you before you begin.

The setup around the work keeps getting in the way. The problem is not you or the idea—it is the shape of how the work is organised.

Does this sound like you?

Chaotic workflowThe project is too bigThe wrong setupConstant distractions

What is often underneath

Overload and decision friction eating the energy needed to start—so the system, not the person, needs the redesign.

07 · Context

Context & Support

The conditions quietly work against it.

The people and conditions around the work undermine it—often invisibly, until you name them.

Does this sound like you?

An unclear briefToo many decision-makersFeedback that stings but doesn't helpMissing support

What is often underneath

Unsafe feedback and environments that punish exploration, which teach you to stop taking creative risks.

+1 · The trapdoor

A Skill Gap

Not a block at all.

A missing capability that mimics a block. It feels like being stuck, but no amount of coaching will move it—because there is nothing hidden to uncover.

Does this sound like you?

I don't actually know how to do this yetIt's not fear—it's a gapI need to learn the craft

What is often underneath

Nothing hidden—this one is honest. The response is teaching or deliberate practice, not coaching, and we say so rather than keeping you inside the service.

Before the label

A door is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

Each name is a starting guess—useful for orientation, but it does not define you or fix what we do next. Real stuckness rarely sits behind a single door: two or three are usually open at once, feeding each other and swapping places over time. A confidence block quietly drains your energy until it starts to look like a process problem.

So we read your state first—flooded, empty or regulated—and follow the symptom back to whatever is driving it before deciding what the work actually needs.

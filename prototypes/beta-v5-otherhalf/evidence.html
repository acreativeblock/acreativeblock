The research we draw on

A multidisciplinary synthesis.

Creative block is under-researched relative to how common it appears to be. There is no agreed scientific taxonomy, and some of the strongest evidence comes from writing, academic and artist samples.

Our framework brings together creative-block research, psychology, coaching, creative practice and systems thinking. Adjacent work on motivation, self-efficacy, mood, avoidance, fixation, incubation, environment and feedback helps us ask better questions—without pretending it directly validates our map.

What we can—and cannot—claim

A useful practitioner method, not a clinical diagnosis.

What we can do

Organise observation, form working hypotheses, and match a proportionate response to the likely mechanism.

What we do not claim

That the framework is a validated diagnostic instrument, that its domains are the final taxonomy, or that one label determines treatment.

How we hold it

As a map that earns its place by being useful—revised as evidence, supervision and practice teach us more.

Boundaries mean scope

Not everything difficult is a creative block.

A specific missing capability may be a skill gap—teach or learn it. Exhaustion may need recovery. A clinical or medical concern needs an appropriately qualified professional. We do not rename these to keep them inside our service.

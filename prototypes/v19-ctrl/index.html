The six blocks

Six kinds of stuck — usually a mix.

01Directional

Too many roads, none chosen. You're not short on ideas — you have too many, and picking one feels like killing the rest. So you keep circling, and call it being stuck.

Sounds like you if… you start everything and finish nothing · you've lost the why · nothing really pulls at you.
The way through — commit to one direction — to test, not forever.
02Structural

The mess is the hiding place. It's not that you don't want to work — you can't find the edge to start at. Tidying feels like progress while the real decision quietly waits.

Sounds like you if… 40 tabs and scattered files · you keep redoing the same step · the plan only lives in your head.
The way through — break the mess into three ordered steps.
03Execution

You know. You just don't. The moment you go to begin, something pulls the handbrake — because avoiding it brings instant relief, and your brain has learned to take it. This isn't laziness.

Sounds like you if… you plan, then avoid the file · starting feels impossible · deadlines keep ambushing you.
The way through — shrink it, and begin for ten ugly minutes.
04Identity

Finishing feels like a verdict. When the work is a test of whether you're good enough, an unfinished piece is safer than a judged one — so you stall at the last ten percent and call it perfectionism.

Sounds like you if… you stall at the finish · you can't hit publish · you compare, constantly.
The way through — ship it imperfect — you are not the work.
05Environmental

Your setup is eating your focus. It isn't discipline. Your space, your hours and your noise spend your energy before you ever reach the work — and willpower can't out-muscle a setup that's working against you.

Sounds like you if… you can't focus where you work · you're drained before you begin · time and space fight you.
The way through — change one condition — space, time or energy.
06Skill

A gap, not a flaw. There's one specific thing the work needs that you haven't learned yet. Because it stays vague, it balloons into “maybe I'm just not good enough” — when really it's just a skill you don't have yet.

Sounds like you if… you hit one technical wall · you binge tutorials · impostor feelings creep in.
The way through — name the one missing skill, and learn just that.

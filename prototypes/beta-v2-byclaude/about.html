About

Built by someone who was stuck — and learned why.

A Creative Block started from a personal question: why is a capable, motivated person still unable to move on the thing that matters? The answer turned out to be a field worth building a practice around.

The founder’s own block

My block was direction, not “can’t paint”.

For years I assumed being “creative” meant being an artist — and that not fitting that mould meant I wasn’t creative at all. So I kept trying to force the wrong shape: more output, more discipline, more pressure. None of it moved the thing that was actually stuck.

What was really blocked was direction. I didn’t need to draw more; I needed to work out what I was for. My strengths are analytic, strategic and systemic — I see how the parts of a problem fit together — and once I named that, the block made sense. It was never a discipline failure. It was the wrong map.

That’s the whole premise here: creativity is bringing something into existence, and the reasons it stalls are specific and knowable. Naming the real one is most of the way out.

Why this practice exists

Evidence-informed, honest, and never woo.

Most “get unstuck” advice is either mystical (unlock your inner artist) or brutal (just do it). Both fail the same way: they ignore that different people, on different terrain, are stuck for different reasons. A Creative Block exists to do the un-glamorous middle — understand the actual cause, say what the evidence does and doesn’t support, and match the response to the person and the work.

What I hold to

The values underneath the work.

These aren’t slogans — they’re the constraints I work inside, and the ones you can hold me to.

Honest about evidence

I use the research carefully and say where it’s thin. This is an evidence-informed practitioner method, not a clinical diagnosis.

Specific, not universal

No single cure. Some terrain is fixed, most is changeable — so the help is matched, never handed out the same to everyone.

Creative ≠ artist

Founders, strategists, researchers and teams get blocked too. Direction and purpose count as much as a blank page.

Where this is

Early, and honest about it.

A Creative Block is a young practice, and I’d rather say so than overclaim. The framework behind it comes from real work: reading the research, drawing on established psychology, coaching and systems thinking, and testing it against actual blocked projects — mine and others’.

It will keep changing as the evidence and the practice do. That’s a feature, not an apology: the map is meant to improve, not to defend its own labels.

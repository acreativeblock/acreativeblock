What makes this different

Most coaches start with systems. We start with why.

Systems and accountability help — until the block comes back. For most creatives the real cause isn't structural, it's psychological: fear, identity, the meaning we attach to the work. I've been there myself, so I know the difference.

Once we've found the root, we build the structure to actually move — depth that ships work, not endless reflection.

The six blocks

Six kinds of stuck. One is yours.

Scroll through each — the feeling, why it keeps coming back, and the way through.

01 · Directional
“I don't know what to make.”

You're not out of ideas — if anything you have too many. As long as you don't choose, every version of you stays possible and none can fail. So the not-choosing isn't laziness, it's protection.

You start everything, finish nothingYou've lost the whyNothing really pulls at you
The way throughCommit to one direction — to test, not forever.
02 · Structural
“It's pure chaos in here.”

The mess is real — but it's also a hiding place. While everything's chaotic you never face the genuinely hard creative decision underneath, so it quietly regenerates every time you get close.

40 tabs, scattered filesYou keep redoing the same stepThe plan lives only in your head
The way throughBreak the mess into three ordered steps.
03 · Execution
“I know what to do — I just don't.”

Avoiding gives instant relief from a threat your nervous system senses — and relief is a reward, so your brain repeats it. You never start, so you never prove it was safe. A loop, not a flaw.

You plan, then avoid the fileStarting feels impossibleDeadlines keep ambushing you
The way throughShrink it. Begin for ten ugly minutes.
04 · Identity
“What if I'm not good enough?”

If your worth rides on the outcome, an unfinished piece is safer than a finished one — it can't be judged. The block is doing a job: keeping your self-image intact. So you stall at the finish.

You stall at the finish lineYou can't hit publishYou compare, constantly
The way throughShip it imperfect — you are not the work.
05 · Environmental
“My life keeps killing my momentum.”

Willpower can't out-muscle a setup that's working against you. When your environment spends your attention and energy first, there's nothing left to push through with — so you read it as failure and try harder, which drains you faster.

You can't focus where you workYou're drained before you beginTime and space fight you
The way throughChange one condition: space, time or energy.
06 · Skill
“I don't know how to do the next bit.”

A vague gap is scarier than a named one. Not knowing exactly what you're missing lets it balloon into “maybe I'm just not good enough,” so you avoid looking — which keeps it vague, which keeps it scary.

You hit one technical wallYou binge tutorialsImpostor feelings creep in
The way throughName the one missing skill — learn just that.

How we work

Understand the block. Match the response.

A Creative Block uses an evidence-informed diagnostic framework to understand why creative work is stuck, then matches the response to the mechanism — coaching, creative consulting, system redesign, skill development or referral.

C

What is stuck, what matters, and what might only look like a creative block?

R

Make the problem workable before demanding more output.

Choose an intervention that fits the likely mechanism.

Form

Turn insight into a decision, experiment or practical change.

T

Test

Try it in real work, learn from the result and adjust.

Not one tool for everything

Different blocks need different help.

Questions of identity, fear or meaning may benefit from coaching. A difficult brief, decision or project may need creative consulting. Repeated workflow friction may need system redesign. A genuine capability gap needs learning. Problems outside our scope need an appropriate referral.

CRAFT is a flexible loop, not a rigid staircase. We form a working hypothesis, test it carefully and revise it when the evidence changes.

Before naming a block

Able + willing + stuck + suffering.

Our working boundary asks whether someone has enough basic capability, genuinely wants or intends to create, cannot begin or continue, and experiences meaningful stuckness because of it.

That prevents us from manufacturing a problem where there may instead be healthy incubation, necessary rest, changed priorities, ordinary difficulty or a real skill gap.

The orienting question

If one thing changed tonight and you could work tomorrow, what would need to be different? Then: what makes that difficult right now?

Four layers

The block is not the whole person.

We separate the terrain around the person, the active mechanism, the professional boundary and the response. That keeps the work specific and avoids treating every obstacle as psychological.

State changes the intervention

Empty, regulated or flooded?

Two people can have the same block and need opposite first steps. Someone flooded by pressure may need lower stakes and a narrower task. Someone empty may need recovery—or gentle activation if they are rested but disengaged.

This is a state dial, not another taxonomy. It tells us how to approach the block, not what the block is. Broader or persistent health concerns sit outside coaching scope and may need qualified support.

Our position

Evidence-informed, not overclaimed.

CRAFT and our map of creative blocks are practitioner syntheses: grounded in research, established theory and creative practice, but not presented as a validated clinical diagnostic system.

Most advice jumps straight to a fix. We start one step earlier—reading what is actually keeping the work stuck—and only then decide what it needs. Here is the whole picture: the evidence we lean on, the terrain, the state, and the CRAFT path we run.

01 · Start with evidence, not a fix

We diagnose before we prescribe.

"Just push through" is the default advice, and for genuinely stuck people it tends to add shame to a problem that was never about willpower. We treat stuckness as information. Before choosing a response we work out what is actually happening—because the same feeling can come from very different mechanisms, and each needs a different first move.1,5

02 · The terrain

What you carry, and what you can change.

Terrain is the background creative work happens against. It changes how vulnerable you are to a block without being the block itself. Some of it is relatively stable; some is genuinely movable—and most of the practical work lives in the movable half.2,3,8

“The view you adopt for yourself profoundly affects the way you lead your life.”Carol Dweck, Mindset
Mostly fixed

Personality

Stable traits shape how you approach uncertainty, evaluation and unfinished work. You cannot out-discipline your own wiring—so we design around it.

How your brain works

Attention, memory and processing differ from person to person. The aim is to work with your mind, not against a template of someone else's.

Mental-health context

Anxiety, low mood and related conditions change the terrain. We stay in scope, and refer on when a block is really something else.9

More movable

Motivation

Why the work matters, and whether the reason still pulls. Motivation can be rebuilt, reconnected, or honestly renegotiated.7

Mood & energy

Your state on the day changes what is possible. Both flat and over-activated states narrow creative thinking.4

Environment

Time, space, interruptions, feedback and the people around the work. Often the fastest change is to the conditions, not the person.2

03 · The state dial

Flooded, empty, or regulated?

The same project can need opposite first moves depending on activation. State is a dial we read before pushing for output—not a block in itself. Too much activation creates threat and rigidity; too little creates flatness and disengagement.4,6

Flooded

Pressure or threat is too high. The first move is to bring it down—not to demand more effort.

Empty

Fuel or engagement is too low. The first move is to restore capacity—not to push through.

Regulated

Enough activation to engage without overwhelm. This is where real creative work happens.

04 · The path

CRAFT—structure without a rigid recipe.

Across a handful of sessions we follow one shape. It organises the enquiry so it stays useful, and adapts to whatever you bring. (CRAFT the method is not "craft", the process-domain problem.)

C

Clarify

What is stuck, what matters, and what may only look like a block?

R

Regulate

Bring the state into range so the problem becomes workable before we demand output.

A

Address

Choose a response that fits the mechanism that is actually maintaining the block.

F

Form

Shape the insight into a concrete decision, experiment or change you can make.

T

Test

Try it in real work, learn from what happens, and adjust—until you can do it without us.10,15

05 · Matching the response

We coach, we consult—or both.

The response is chosen, not assumed. Some blocks need reflective coaching; some need hands-on work on the project; many need a blend. A few need something else entirely—learning a missing skill, redesigning a system, recovery, or a referral. There is no honour in using the wrong tool consistently.

Coaching

For the person: fear of exposure, confidence, direction, meaning and a difficult relationship with the work.

Creative consulting

For the project: briefs, decisions, scope, process and workflow—the practical shape of the work.

Teach, redesign or refer

A real skill gap gets taught;17 a broken system gets redesigned; a clinical concern is referred to the right professional.

Where the evidence stops

Honest about what this is.

Creative block is under-researched relative to how common it appears to be. There is no agreed scientific taxonomy, and some of the strongest evidence comes from writing, academic and artist samples.5,11

Our framework brings together creative-block research, psychology, coaching, creative practice and systems thinking. Adjacent work on motivation, self-efficacy, mood, avoidance, fixation, incubation, environment and feedback helps us ask better questions—without pretending it directly validates our map.

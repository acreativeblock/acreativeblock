The grief no one names — why “not being creative anymore” is often a loss, not a lack.

Blog · Essay · 2026 · 07 · 09

The grief no one names.

Six minutes. On childhood play, quiet disengagement, and what to do with a loss you were never allowed to mourn.

Watch a child make something. They don't ask whether they're allowed, whether it's good, whether they're “a creative.” They just play. Then somewhere along the way they're told, gently or not, that play isn't for grown-ups — and they file it away as one of the small deaths of growing up.

Most people never mourn it. There's no funeral for the version of you that used to draw for hours, or fill notebooks, or build things no one asked for. It just quietly stops. And years later, when you try to come back to the work, you find a door that won't open, and you assume the problem is discipline.

It usually isn't. What looks like “I'm not creative anymore” is often an unprocessed loss — and loss doesn't respond to productivity. You can't schedule your way out of grief. You can only name it, and let it be real.

That's the first move we make. Not a plan, not a system. Just the honest recognition that something was set down, and that picking it back up is allowed. The stuck loosens the moment it stops being treated as a character flaw and starts being treated as what it is.

And you don't do that part alone. Naming a loss in the company of someone who takes it seriously is half the work. The rest — the structure, the steps, the way back — comes after, and comes easier.

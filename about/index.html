About A Creative Block

We were told we were creative. Nobody told us what to do with it.

A Creative Block is a coaching and consulting platform for creatives who are stuck—on a project, on a career, or on the question of who they actually are.

Not the tidy kind of stuck you solve by “just making something.” The other kind. The kind that lasts years.

Why we exist

The block is rarely about the work.

Most advice for blocked creatives says the same thing: start painting, start writing, do it badly, play more. Sometimes that is exactly right. Sometimes the project needs a process, a deadline, more confidence or better working conditions.

But often the block sits somewhere else entirely.

01

In identity

Trying to merge who you are with a creative job you admired, rather than one that fits you.

02

In the label

“Creative” arrives with a costume and a set of expectations. If you do not match them, the whole thing can start to feel like a fraud.

03

In the conditions

Talented people spend their best years on work that asks too little of them, taking briefs from people who do not understand what the work needs.

04

In isolation

A block is often easier to move through by talking. Brainstorming needs a second brain.

Too many creatives burn out or leave the work, while specific research and support remain surprisingly thin. That gap is why we are here.

Who is behind it

Founded by someone who was blocked for fifteen years.

A Creative Block was founded by Mirco, a designer based in Lisbon. The short version: he spent roughly fifteen years trying to become a creative, and only recently worked out he had been looking in the wrong direction the whole time.

He studied product design, arriving at university knowing none of the names his classmates already knew, and spent the first years catching up. He moved into graphic design because it paid. He went deep on typography, branding, posters, street art and Swiss design systems.

Then came the search: music, dance, photography, documentary, writing, web design, apps and DJing. A camper bought so he could shoot on film and develop it in the bathroom while living on the road. A magazine that never launched. A wall painted as a blackboard, covered in chalk maps of his own personality. Ikigai sessions, therapy, creative coaching, every productivity system and note-taking app in existence.

For thirteen of those years, when people asked how life was, the answer was always the same: good, but something is missing.

The missing piece was not discipline. It was trying to force himself into a version of “creative” that was never his.

The turn

Deleting everything and starting from strengths.

Not “what do I want to be?” but “what already comes naturally?”

Last year he deleted all of it. Every note, every map, every system built to answer the question of who he was.

What came back was consistent. People had always come to him—classmates asking him to look at their projects, friends still calling years later for a reference, a book or a second opinion. People brought him problems with partners and family. People said he made a room feel safe to be yourself in.

He is the person who walks into a room and looks for whoever nobody is talking to. He gets genuinely angry when people he cares about are not thriving. He finds self-discovery and growth to be the most interesting part of being alive.

That turned out to be the creative work: building systems, understanding people and making something that did not exist before. It is what puts him in a flow state, fourteen or fifteen hours at a stretch.

Creativity is not a medium. It is bringing something into being with what you actually have.

What we believe

A few things we will not ask you to pretend.

01

You do not have to fit the costume. Creative is not a look, a personality type or a list of approved disciplines.

02

Your block deserves diagnosis, not a slogan. Perfectionism, impostor feelings, the wrong environment and the wrong identity are different problems. They need different answers.

03

Nobody unblocks alone. Brainstorming requires a second brain. Accountability requires a witness.

04

Your brain is not broken; your system might be. Sometimes the shift is as small as finding a tool that works the way you actually think.

05

You will be supported, not shamed. Nobody here will tell you what a creative is supposed to look like.

How we work

Support for the person and the work.

A Creative Block offers coaching and consulting for creatives—whether the goal is moving a project forward, rebuilding a working process or rethinking what kind of creative you are in the first place.

The work is grounded in trained practice, not only lived experience. Mirco is an MOE-certified coach and a member of the Association for Coaching.

Where we are going

One coach is the starting point.

The intention is a network—coaches, therapists and consultants—and eventually an organisation that treats creative mental health as a serious subject. Somewhere between a school, a residency and a foundation. A place creatives go when the work stops working.

The original idea still stands, and may return: creatives meeting regularly, each with their own project. State the goal. Work for a few hours. Check in. Talk it through. Suffer together, which is much better than suffering alone.
